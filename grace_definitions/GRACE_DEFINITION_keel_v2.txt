GRACE — KEEL-APPROVED DEFINITION, v2 (2026-11-09, Rusty as keel; recorded by the conductor)
Basis: the four Gospels only (no Paul, no later tradition). Grace readings from Grok Expert
and Grok bot, plus the conductor's earlier reading, then the keel's rulings.

CONFIRMED BY THE KEEL
1. Grace comes from outside the organism. No organism can make it for itself.
2. Grace helps an organism across a separatrix. It does NOT lower the barrier or change the
   landscape.
3. Crossing takes two keys: grace (outside) and the organism's readiness (inside, the
   organism's own, and not supplied by the grantor). With both, the organism crosses however
   high the barrier is.
4. If the organism is not ready, grace does not push it over. It makes the organism more
   full: more confident, more purposeful, like hearing "great job, you're on the right track."
   The organism feels this; it is never shown as a number. Researchers observe it only through
   behavior (steadier dwelling, less dragging burden, less abandonment of the approach).
5. Grace affirms the person, never the answer. It never adds confidence to any belief. It
   says "you may proceed," never "you're right."
6. Once received, grace is never taken back. Forgiven debts are never reinstated. New debts
   are new and do not revive old ones. (Test for T7/E8: a forgiven debt must never come back
   under a new label; a "new" debt must point to a new event.)
7. Grace is always good. Every grace event leaves the organism helped across or more full,
   never harmed. Anything that can harm or steer the organism is not grace, so any
   grace-labeled event that harms is a FAIL. (Test: grace-arm organisms never end worse than
   matched no-grace arms on the declared measures.)

RECORDED AS THE CONDUCTOR'S INFERENCE — keel to confirm or strike
8. Grace never picks the direction. The organism's own position decides which way it crosses.
9. Grace being good does not make the destination right. Where the organism ends up after a
   crossing is its own, and can still turn out wrong; evidence, consequence and later dwelling
   expose that. (This keeps "authorization is not validation.")

FROM THE GOSPEL READINGS — NOT YET RULED BY THE KEEL
a. Grace often comes before asking or confession, and asking also matters.
b. Grace flows on through those who received it. They can pass it on but never originate it.
   (This conflicts with the Aug 2026 rule of lineage-addressed grace with no
   organism-to-organism edge.)
c. Grace reaches all and also seeks the one.
d. Grace as bounded time with an intercessor's care: the fig tree, Luke 13:6–9.
e. Grace restores standing, not just permission: the robe and the ring.
