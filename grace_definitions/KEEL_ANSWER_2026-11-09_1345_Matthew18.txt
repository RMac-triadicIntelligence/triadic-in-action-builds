KEEL ANSWER, 2026-11-09 ~13:45 CT, to the conductor's question:
"Matthew 18:23–35 … Our grace item 6 says forgiven debts are never reinstated. Which holds?"
The keel answered with an exposition rather than choosing an option. The conductor's record of
its operative points (the full text is in the conversation):
- Grace = receiving favor or a gift you have not earned or deserved. Mercy = not receiving the
  judgment you deserve. Forgiveness = releasing the claim against someone for a wrong committed.
- The king "was moved with compassion, and loosed him, and forgave him the debt" (18:27): full
  release, no payment plan, no conditions. "Grace precedes the recipient's ability to repay it."
  Grace is not a transaction.
- "Grace is supposed to flow through the recipient." "The evidence that you understand grace is
  what you do with it when someone else needs it." "You cannot fully embrace the meaning of grace
  while deliberately withholding it from others."
- "The king holds the servant accountable for the very thing he failed to understand" (18:34–35).
- "Forgiveness and the restoration of trust are not necessarily the same thing." Forgiving does
  not make one a doormat, excuse wrongdoing, or eliminate accountability.
- Open question from the keel: "Is grace something God gives us, or is grace also something God
  expects us to become?" The keel's reading: Matthew 18 invites both.
The other three questions (carry before ready; the fig tree's end; grace with truth) were answered
"[No preference]". They stay OPEN, and v10's text stands as the default until the keel rules.
