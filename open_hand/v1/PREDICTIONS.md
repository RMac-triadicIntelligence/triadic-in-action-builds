# open_hand toy: written before the first run (2026-11-10, about 07:10 CT)

The keel's statement under test (07:06): "When you have an open hand and rain falls, there is spill
over. You can't hold it all. The spillover is for the community."

The toy: 60 members on a ring. Each has a daily need and a hand of limited size; needs and hand sizes
differ. Rain falls unevenly: half the ring is wet, half is nearly dry. Same rain in every arm.
- OPEN: a hand holds only up to its size. Whatever is over runs to the two neighbours.
- ASKED: no limit on holding. A member in want asks a neighbour every step, and the neighbour always
  gives half of what it holds. (The rule from my commons model, made as favourable as I can.)
- BARNS: no limit on holding. Nobody gives.
Measured: how often members go without ("want"), who they are, and what happens when the rain stops.

What a toy can and cannot do: it shows what follows from these rules. It proves nothing about grace.

Predictions I commit to
- OH1. With plenty of rain overall (1.3x total need), OPEN has less want than BARNS.
- OH2. In OPEN, members in the dry half are fed nearly as well as members in the wet half.
- OH3. In BARNS, the dry half stays in want however much rain falls on the wet half.

What I do not know (no prediction)
- U1. OPEN against ASKED. Willing giving when asked may do just as well as spilling.
- U2. Scarcity (rain 0.8x need): who goes without under OPEN? Spread evenly, or the ones far from rain?
- U3. Drought (rain stops): OPEN keeps no personal reserve. Does it fail sooner than BARNS?
- U4. Built in, not findings: total rain is the same in all arms, and nothing is created or lost.
