RT-F1. H1 to H4 could not have failed. MAIN FINDING.
The frozen minimums are 10, 5, 2 and 2 points. The effects are fixed by the rules:
- H1: ALONE can answer only one-condition questions on its own two sensors, 0.2 x 2/6 = 6.67% (observed 6.75% answered). FULL beating that is arithmetic.

The contract says the usefulness of complementary information is designed in. The verdict "SUPPORTED_IN_THIS_INSTRUMENT" four times still reads as four tests passed. The one hypothesis that could fail, did. Suggested fix: relabel H1 to H4 as structural checks, as you already do for the label controls, and keep "hypothesis" for claims that can fail.

RT-F2. H5 tested a nearly inert channel; the benefit lives in what was withheld.
The basis only reweights which missing condition to ask about first. All missing conditions are needed anyway. Probe P2: inheritance changes 2,280 of 17,280 events and the answer in 368.
What carries the within-life gain (63.8% to 81.4%) is the who-senses-what table, and children are born without it. Probe P6b (mine, post hoc, not a retune of your run): if children also inherit that table by slot, the early-descendant window goes from 64.0% to 82.4%.
So "inherited salience did not help" is correct, and it says little about inheritance. Note the tension for the keel: that table is relational structure keyed to individuals, which your contract excludes as too close to biography. Which structures may cross a generation is the keel's ruling, not ours.

RT-F3. SHUFFLED_TERRAIN is a misleading map, not a neutral one; and one arm is reported twice.
Rotating the coordinate by one makes the table point at channels peers lack with weight 0.98 squared. It scores below random (39.9% vs 46.6%). H4's +35 points is "true map vs false map". The neutral comparison is H3's +28.5.
FROZEN_TERRAIN and RANDOM_CONTACT are event-identical in all 17,280 events (P1). The report lists them as two results.

RT-F4. The community cannot catch a mistake, and the contract implies it can.
The focal only ever asks for conditions it has no observation of, so no condition is ever observed twice: 0 of 17,280 FULL events (P3). "Contradictory bits leave UNKNOWN" is never exercised. Wrong answers among answered are 9.28%, against 9.46% predicted from 4% noise with no cross-checking (P5). That is why correlated error yields 28.7% wrong. A society that can be wrong together and cannot notice is a real result; it should be stated as a design limit.

RT-F5. Reliability learning is inert, and debts punish noise.
With trust held constant, FULL scores 74.95% vs 75.13% (P6a). Errors are independent per event, so a provider's past error predicts nothing. Debts arise from that same noise. The forgiveness contrast (1.7 points) therefore measures only the authored willingness penalty, as you say.

The keel's definition, given after your build (2026-11-09 19:51): "A perfect society is one that grows from the members' own reaching and their interactions together."
